00 / Statement

Research statement

My interest began with a question that bioenergy forces you to take seriously: what does it take for a fuel pathway to be more than technically possible?

In the sustainable aviation fuel studies I contributed to, the binding constraints were rarely chemical on their own. Feedstock had to be available in the right place at the right cost, the conversion technology had to suit the scale of the proposed refinery, byproducts needed somewhere to go, and the whole proposition had to survive a financial model and a regulatory review. Holding those constraints together — without letting any one of them quietly set the answer — is the part of process engineering I find most interesting, and it is where I would like to do doctoral work.

The laboratory side came first, and it shaped how I work. My undergraduate research on glycerol polymerisation — published in the ASEAN Journal of Chemical Engineering in 2024 — was a study of severity: whether a reaction defined by high temperature and long residence time could be made milder by changing the catalytic medium rather than the temperature. Asked at bench scale, with an HPLC and a Dean–Stark apparatus, it is the same question I meet at plant scale in techno-economic work.

Between the two sits a habit of measurement. Working inside a pilot workshop producing ternary precursors for battery materials taught me how much of research quality is decided by operational discipline: reproducible batches, documented procedures, drawings that match the building.

My doctoral interest sits between those experiences: the assessment and optimisation of low-carbon fuel and material pathways. The method I want to work with combines process modelling and techno-economic analysis with software tooling that makes such analyses transparent, repeatable and usable by the people who run the plant.

This is a direction rather than a completed programme of research, and I am looking for a supervisor who would help me shape it into a tractable thesis.

01 / Peer-reviewed

Peer-reviewed publication

One article, published open access. First author, developed with three co-authors at Institut Teknologi Bandung.

ASEAN Journal of Chemical Engineering Vol. 24, No. 3 pp. 353–364 2024 Peer-reviewed

Synthesis of Polyglycerol under Superbasic Conditions to Reduce the Severity of the Reaction Conditions

Malikul Mulki Anas Suyadi1,2, Giovanni Andre Halim1,2, Tatang Hernas Soerawidjaja1,2, Jenny Rizkiana1,2,3,*

1 Department of Chemical Engineering, Institut Teknologi Bandung 2 Department of Bioenergy Engineering and Chemurgy, Institut Teknologi Bandung 3 Center for Catalysis and Reaction Engineering, Institut Teknologi Bandung * Corresponding author: Jenny Rizkiana

Submitted 25 July 2024 Accepted 17 December 2024 Published 31 December 2024 DOI 10.22146/ajche.15164 CC BY-NC 4.0

Abstract

The increasing biodiesel production in Indonesia will affect the supply of glycerol because it is a by-product of the transesterification reaction. Abundant glycerol can provide added value if further processed into higher-value products such as polyglycerol. Polyglycerol is made from the polymerization of glycerol with the help of an alkaline catalyst, such as KOH, but the temperature and reaction time used are very high and long. Therefore, this study aims to increase the conversion of glycerol by adding DMSO to the glycerol and KOH solution to increase the basicity of the mixture, increase the polymerization rate, and decrease the temperature and reaction time. The conversion of glycerol to polyglycerol with the addition of 10 mL DMSO to the KOH and glycerol mixture, giving a DMSO to KOH-glycerol volume ratio of 1:5, with a reaction time of 2 hours and a reaction temperature of 140-150°C was 77%, compared to without the addition of KOH which required a reaction time of 8 hours and a reaction temperature of 260-280 °C resulting in a glycerol conversion of 99%. Adding DMSO, with constant KOH-glycerol volume, affects the polymerization reaction because compared to adding 5 mL DMSO (volume ratio of 1:10), the resulting glycerol conversion is 16%. For the effect of reaction time on the results of glycerol polymerization, 5 mL DMSO shows that the longer the time, the more polyglycerol formed can depolymerize into short-chain oligomers or back into glycerol. The optimum reaction time for 5 mL DMSO is 6 hours because the glycerol conversion is 63%, producing diglycerol and triglycerol with 30% and 17% selectivities, respectively.

Keywords Diglycerol DMSO Glycerol KOH Polyglycerol

What the paper contributes

  1. A superbasic KOH/DMSO medium for glycerol condensation polymerisation, used in place of a higher temperature.
  2. A working point at 140–150 °C and 2 hours instead of 260–280 °C and 8 hours — a temperature at which KOH alone gave no conversion at all.
  3. A selectivity map: short-chain oligomers with 5 mL DMSO over 6 hours, longer chains with 10 mL DMSO.

Academic theses · 2

MSc thesis · 2024 · Chemical Engineering, Institut Teknologi Bandung

Evaluation of the Effectiveness of a Calcium Acetate Monohydrate–Glycerol–Methanol Catalyst (Catalyst X) in Polyglycerol Production

Original title (Indonesian) Evaluasi Keefektifan Katalis Kalsium Asetat Monohidrat–Gliserol–Metanol (Katalis X) dalam Produksi Poligliserol

Supervisor: Jenny Rizkiana, S.T., M.T., Ph.D. Faculty of Industrial Technology, Institut Teknologi Bandung

Summary

The thesis examined the catalytic activity of Catalyst X, synthesised from calcium acetate monohydrate, glycerol and methanol, for producing polyglycerol from glycerol — a calcium-based alternative to the alkali–DMSO route explored in the published undergraduate work.

Methods

  • Catalyst synthesis and characterisation by FTIR and XRD.
  • Evaluation of catalytic activity and glycerol conversion.
  • Effect of catalyst-to-glycerol ratio and reaction time.
  • Analysis of product distribution and oligomer selectivity.

Key findings

  • 38.32% maximum glycerol conversion, at a catalyst-to-glycerol ratio of 1:25 and a reaction time of 8 hours.
  • Selectivity of 27.47% toward PG2, 6.87% toward PG3 and 1.07% toward PG4+.
  • Polyglycerol with visibly better colour and odour than the KOH/DMSO route.

BSc final project · 2023 · Bioenergy and Chemurgy Engineering, Institut Teknologi Bandung

Synthesis of Polyglycerol Using KOH-DMSO and K2CO3-ZnO Catalysts: Investigation of Reaction Time, KOH-DMSO Concentration and K2CO3-ZnO Composition

Original title (Indonesian) Sintesis Poligliserol dengan Katalis KOH-DMSO & K2CO3-ZnO: Investigasi Waktu Reaksi, Konsentrasi KOH-DMSO, dan Komposisi K2CO3-ZnO

Supervisor: Jenny Rizkiana, S.T., M.T., Ph.D. Faculty of Industrial Technology, Institut Teknologi Bandung

Summary

The final project investigated polyglycerol synthesis from glycerol across two catalytic systems, KOH-DMSO and K2CO3-ZnO, aiming at more efficient production at lower operating temperature and shorter reaction time. It is the experimental foundation of the 2024 peer-reviewed article.

Methods

  • KOH-DMSO catalytic system, including the effect of DMSO concentration.
  • Effect of reaction time on polyglycerol production.
  • K2CO3-ZnO catalyst composition as a solid, alkali-free alternative.
  • Analysis of glycerol conversion and oligomer distribution.

Key findings

  • 51.10% diglycerol-to-hexaglycerol yield after 8 hours with 5 mL DMSO.
  • 25.23% diglycerol-to-hexaglycerol and 58.98% long-chain polyglycerol after 2 hours with 10 mL DMSO.
  • The K2CO3-ZnO system reached no glycerol conversion under the conditions investigated.
